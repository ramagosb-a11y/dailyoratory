# REDESIGN IDEAS — Bible as a Living Reading Companion

Feature ID: `bible-welcoming-scripture-guide`
Revision: `REDESIGN-IDEAS-R2` / 2026-09-19
Status: exploratory — not approved for implementation

## Design direction

Keep the completed simplified page as the foundation. The next iteration should make a reader feel they have a clear, low-pressure next step rather than adding back a catalog of Catholic tools.

```text
Welcome and one next action
            ↓
Choose a passage for today
            ↓
Read · Notice · Respond
            ↓
Slow down with Lectio Divina
            ↓
Understand where the book belongs
```

## Recommended modules

### 1. Start with a passage

Place immediately after the hero. Offer three equal, non-tracked routes:

- **Read today’s Gospel** — the existing official USCCB daily readings path, clearly labeled as a Catholic option.
- **Begin with a Gospel** — a gentle invitation to begin with one Gospel in sequence, without a forced plan.
- **Read a Psalm** — an invitation for readers who want poetry, praise, lament, or reflection.

Each card needs one sentence explaining why it is useful and an external or internal destination that is verified before implementation. Do not ask the user to select an identity, save progress, or make a commitment.

### 2. A reading companion, not a study system

Replace the five general suggestion cards with a visually stronger three-step sequence:

1. **Read the passage** — begin with what the words actually say.
2. **Notice the context** — who is speaking, what happened before, and what kind of writing this is.
3. **Respond honestly** — hold a question, write a short note, talk with someone, or pray if that is part of the reader’s practice.

Add a small “when you get stuck” note: reread the larger section and use a trustworthy study resource or community. Avoid positioning the page as a source of definitive personal spiritual answers.

### 3. Make the Lectio panel the emotional center

Keep the existing navy/gold heavenly treatment, but open it with one sentence that explains its benefit in plain language: it gives readers permission to slow down rather than race through a chapter. Preserve its optionality; a reader may reflect, write, sit quietly, or pray.

### 4. Give the Bible map a clearer purpose

Add one “What will I find here?” line to each collection card and one suggested entry point. The map should help readers choose a genre and understand that the Bible is a library, not a linear book to master in one pass. Avoid counts or canon comparisons without a dedicated source review.

### 5. End with a quiet return invitation

Replace a generic resource grid with a calm, one-card conclusion:

> Return to the same passage tomorrow, or choose the next chapter. The goal is not to finish quickly; it is to keep making room to read attentively.

This is original, non-doctrinal encouragement and needs no user data or completion state.

## Visual redesign

- Retain the parchment hero, but add a simple “open page” visual rhythm through a wide first-step card rather than imagery or a new asset.
- Use one strong visual contrast moment only: the existing dark Lectio panel. All other sections should remain parchment/ivory, generous, and easy to scan.
- Use three pathway cards, then a three-step reading sequence, then the dark Lectio pause, then four Bible-map cards. This creates a readable cadence of 3 → 3 → 6 → 4 rather than a wall of equal boxes.
- At narrow widths, stack every card and keep actions full-width. Preserve the established 44px target size and focus ring.

## Content and review boundary

The revised public copy would add formation language about beginning with the Gospels/Psalms, the role of context, and the reading flow. Before implementation, create an exact content revision, verify each external route/resource, and obtain fresh Catholic Sources, Formation, independent Theology, UX, and SEO review for the affected content. Privacy remains risk-reducing if the redesign stays free of input, saved progress, sharing, and analytics changes.

## Explicit non-goals

- Do not restore local selectors, journals, copy/print actions, reading-plan tracking, FAQs, resource directories, or a translation recommendation engine.
- Do not quote Bible text or make promises that a passage supplies a certain personal answer.
- Do not replace or modify the existing Mass Readings Reflections page.
