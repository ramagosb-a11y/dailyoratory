## CF-C-03 — Orientation

- Eyebrow: `Begin with context`
- Heading: `How to read the Fathers well`
- Intro: `Read the Fathers as important historical witnesses and teachers within the Church's living Tradition. Scripture is the inspired word of God; the Fathers help us hear how Christians in earlier centuries received, discussed, prayed, and lived the faith.`
- Three guide points:
  1. `Read more than a line.` — `A passage has an argument, audience, and moment in history. Let the work speak in its own setting.`
  2. `Do not make one writer carry everything.` — `No single Father settles every question. Look for context, reception, and the Church's teaching.`
  3. `Keep a trusted guide nearby.` — `A good edition, sound catechesis, and the Church's living teaching help a difficult text become a real encounter rather than a slogan.`
- Source map: CF-R-01, CF-R-02. The historical-setting language is an original caution, not a claim about any one author.

## CF-C-04 — Question doors

### Topic-browser framing — CONTENT-R1.1

- Eyebrow: `Browse by topic`
- Heading: `Follow one honest question`
- Intro: `Choose Scripture, worship, Christ, or Christian life. You do not need to survey an entire era before beginning.`
- Scope note: These four topic labels point only to the four reviewed doors below; they do not claim that any one writer exhaustively represents the topic.

### Door A

- Question: `How have Christians learned to read Scripture?`
- Guide: `Start with Book I, chapters 1–5 of Augustine's *On Christian Doctrine*. Read this short opening, notice the question he is pursuing, then return to Scripture itself.`
- Action: `Open Book I, chapters 1–5` → `https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/12021.htm`
- Source map: CF-R-06, CF-R-01.

### Door B

- Question: `What can an early source show us about Christian worship?`
- Guide: `Start with chapters 65–67 of Justin Martyr's *First Apology*. Read them as one writer's witness in a particular setting, then notice what they say and what they do not attempt to cover.`
- Action: `Open First Apology` → `https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0126.htm`
- Source map: CF-R-04.

### Door C

- Question: `How did an early Christian writer speak about Christ?`
- Guide: `Start with Athanasius's *On the Incarnation of the Word*. Take one section at a time, and keep the question close: what is this author trying to help his readers confess?`
- Action: `Open On the Incarnation` → `https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm`
- Source map: CF-R-05.

### Door D

- Question: `What did an early Christian writing ask of everyday life?`
- Guide: `Start with the *Didache*. It is an early Christian writing, not the work of a named Church Father. Read its moral instruction in context; do not treat its historical practices as a direct modern rule.`
- Action: `Open The Didache` → `https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0714.htm`
- Source map: CF-R-07.

## CF-C-05 — First reading pathway

- Eyebrow: `Take one unhurried step`
- Heading: `A first reading path`
- Intro: `Choose one door. Open the text. Read a small portion. If something is unclear, mark the question and return with a trustworthy guide. You do not need to master an era in one sitting.`
- Steps:
  1. `Choose the question that is genuinely yours.`
  2. `Read a short section before following links or searching for conclusions.`
  3. `Name one question to carry into Scripture, catechesis, or conversation with a trusted teacher.`
- Note: `The linked repositories use particular translations and editorial framing. They are study aids; read the page's source information and the work's context before drawing broad conclusions.`
- Source map: CF-R-03 through CF-R-07; original formation guidance.
